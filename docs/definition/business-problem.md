# Business Problem

Modern software development relies on multiple specialized security tools to identify vulnerabilities throughout the Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC). However, these tools often operate independently and produce reports in different formats, making it difficult for development and security teams to obtain a unified view of the security status of their projects.

This creates several challenges:

* **Fragmented security information:** Findings are distributed across multiple tools and platforms.
* **Inconsistent reporting formats:** Security tools may produce results using different formats and data structures.
* **Limited visibility:** Teams lack a centralized view of vulnerabilities across their projects.
* **Increased operational overhead:** Developers and security teams must manually review and manage findings from multiple sources.
* **Difficulty tracking vulnerabilities:** Without centralized historical data, it is harder to determine whether vulnerabilities have been resolved, remain open, or reappear.
* **Tool integration complexity:** Integrating and maintaining multiple security tools independently increases development and maintenance effort.

As a result, organizations may spend significant time consolidating security information instead of focusing on vulnerability remediation and improving the security of their software.

The business problem is therefore the **lack of a centralized and standardized way to collect, normalize, track, and visualize security findings from multiple tools throughout the SDLC**.
